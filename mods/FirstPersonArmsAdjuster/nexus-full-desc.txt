[center][color=#C94A4A][size=6]First Person Arms Adjuster[/size][/color]
[i]Get weapons away from your face and add camera-only movement without changing FOV or aim.[/i][/center]

[line]

[b]First Person Arms Adjuster[/b] lets you reposition the first-person body, arms, and held equipment on screen, reduce animation clipping, and add configurable head bob without changing combat or aiming.

Use it to push an oversized viewmodel farther away, bring it closer, or make small horizontal and vertical corrections without replacing the game's field of view.

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Features[/size][/color][/heading]
[list]
[*][b]Reposition your first-person view:[/b] Move your body, arms, weapons, shields, and held arrows farther away, closer, left, right, up, or down.[/*]
[*][b]Tune each equipment type:[/b] Set separate depth positions for melee weapons, bows, and magic, with one general position for everything else.[/*]
[*][b]Reduce body and weapon clipping:[/b] Apply automatic corrections during normal melee attacks, dodges, all-equipment sheathing, bow draw, and bonfire interactions.[/*]
[*][b]Advanced head-bob settings:[/b] Add smooth movement to your view while controlling how strongly it appears against your arms. Choose Subtle, Balanced, or Strong, adjust its gait, and use temporal-safe rendering to prevent unwanted blur.[/*]
[*][b]Keep everything visually connected:[/b] Multi-part weapons, held equipment, and supported visual effects remain aligned with your hands during movement and animation.[/*]
[*][b]Change presentation without changing gameplay:[/b] Adjustments apply immediately without affecting FOV, aiming, projectiles, hit detection, physics, or player movement.[/*]
[/list]

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Positioning[/size][/color][/heading]
Offsets are measured in meters relative to the camera:

[list]
[*]Positive [b]ForwardOffset[/b] moves the first-person model farther from the camera.[/*]
[*]Positive [b]HorizontalOffset[/b] moves it to the right.[/*]
[*]Positive [b]VerticalOffset[/b] moves it upward.[/*]
[/list]

Category offsets are enabled by default. Melee weapons, bows, and magic each use 0.30. Start with forward values between 0.20 and 0.35. Set every offset to 0 or disable the plugin to restore the vanilla presentation.

If a first-person pose makes the torso or shoulders visible, [b]Shoulder Retraction[/b] pulls that region back toward or behind the camera while preserving the configured hand and weapon position. Its default 0.05 metre correction is concentrated around the upper chest and shoulders, then tapers through the arms to zero at the hands. During a dodge, FPAA can smoothly strengthen this correction, keep it active through rapid chained dodges, and restore the normal value afterward.

The [b]Advanced - Retraction Profile[/b] section provides separate controls for the spine, left and right shoulders, upper arms, and forearms. Each region can use up to twice the master retraction distance for difficult armor or animation combinations, while the hands remain fixed.

Additional off-by-default controls cover the lower torso, chest helpers, shoulder helpers, and native cloth used by armor that does not follow the standard upper-body profile. They can be tuned independently without changing the hand or weapon position.

[b]Torso Renderer Retraction[/b] handles complete torso garments that do not respond to ordinary shoulder or spine adjustments. Its independent weight defaults to half of the master Shoulder Retraction distance and leaves the arms, hands, weapons, and unrelated cloth at their normal positions.

For unusual armor, Diagnostics can identify additional first-person bones and renderers. [b]Test Retraction Bone Name[/b] and [b]Test Bone Retraction[/b] provide a live one-at-a-time test before you commit to a permanent profile; avoid testing hand, finger, or weapon-socket bones.

[b]Advanced - Animation Guards[/b] consolidates automatic correction for melee attacks, dodges, all-equipment sheathing, bow draw, and held-melee poses. A shared Move Toward Vanilla target keeps overlapping attack and sheathing guards predictable instead of stacking their effects. Dodge guarding strengthens only Shoulder Retraction and remains covered through rapid chains. Bow guarding follows nocking smoothly, holds its configured depth limit while aiming, then restores the normal bow position gradually after firing or cancellation. The default 33 percent ceiling limits the default 0.30 metre Bow Depth Offset to about 0.10 metres while drawing.

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Head Bob[/size][/color][/heading]
The game's vanilla first-person head bob moves the same arm and body rig that FPAA repositions. With adjusted arms, this can pull shoulders and torso into the camera and cause severe clipping.

FPAA therefore suppresses the vanilla first-person bob and replaces it with custom camera movement. Adjustable render-only viewmodel follow lets arms and held visuals share part of that translation, while an optional stabilization toggle makes them follow it exactly, without changing aiming, camera depth, gameplay transforms, or third person.

The game's [b]Accessibility / Head Bob[/b] toggle remains the global master switch. FPAA provides its own local toggle, Subtle/Balanced/Strong presets, adjustable smoothness, sprint emphasis, gait speed, viewmodel follow, and viewmodel stabilization. Temporal-safe timing is enabled by default to prevent the camera movement from smearing through TAA or temporal upscaling, while optional motion-blur suppression is available for players who still find bob uncomfortable.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Rendering Scope And Limitations[/size][/color][/heading]
Head bob adds vertical and side-to-side first-person camera movement based on distance travelled and sprinting. Presets control overall strength, while Speed, Smoothness, and Sprint Emphasis tune the gait. Viewmodel Follow controls how much the arms share that movement, and stabilization can keep arms, weapons, and supported effects visually steady against the bob. Native movement authored into weapon animations remains untouched.

Temporal Safe Head Bob Timing prevents TAA and temporal upscalers from turning the camera movement into a full-screen smear. Optional motion-blur suppression removes only the camera blur caused by visible FPAA bob while preserving blur on moving objects. Neither feature changes rendering quality or adds another render pass.

Animation guards cover normal melee attacks, rapid or redirected dodges, sheathing for every equipment type, and bow draw. Overlapping attack and sheathing guards use the strongest requested correction rather than multiplying together. Dodge correction affects only the exposed shoulder and torso region. Bow correction arrives smoothly while nocking, remains stable while aiming, and yields gradually after the shot. These guards do not change animation timing, aim, or projectile origin.

All corrections are visual. FPAA leaves the gameplay skeleton, aiming, attacks, colliders, physics, movement, and projectiles unchanged. Supported held effects remain aligned with the adjusted equipment, and [b]Adjust Attached Effects[/b] can disable that correction independently.

Released arrows originate from the unchanged gameplay fire point, so a small visual transition can remain when a held arrow becomes a projectile.

Treat unusually large offsets as experimental. Test melee weapons, bows, shields, magic, item use, swimming, and mounted gameplay when choosing a profile.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Updating[/size][/color][/heading]
Incompatible config updates create a backup and generate current defaults. Some supported settings are automatically carried over.

FoA Mod Manager's final [b]Import Previous Settings[/b] tab shows the current and available backup schemas. Toggle the import action to restore compatible customized settings. Restart the game after importing.

Durable customized settings are preserved when they remain compatible, including positioning, retraction profiles, equipment depth, head bob, animation guards, effect alignment, and diagnostics. One-shot actions and retired settings are not imported.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Troubleshooting[/size][/color][/heading]
If arms or equipment appear clipped, hidden, or too distant, reduce the active category offset first. Set all offsets to 0 or disable [b]Enabled[/b] to confirm the vanilla presentation before testing another value.

Enable [b]Diagnostics[/b] to investigate first-person render paths, active offsets, head bob, attached effects, and animation-guard state. Full details are written to [b]BepInEx/LogOutput.log[/b].

[center][color=#C94A4A][size=6]First Person Arms Adjuster[/size][/color]
[i]Get weapons away from your face and add camera-only movement without changing FOV or aim.[/i][/center]

[line]

[b]First Person Arms Adjuster[/b] lets you reposition the first-person body, arms, and held equipment on screen, reduce animation clipping, and add configurable head bob without changing combat or aiming.

Use it to push an oversized viewmodel farther away, bring it closer, or make small horizontal and vertical corrections without replacing the game's field of view.

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Features[/size][/color][/heading]
[list]
[*][b]Reposition your first-person view:[/b] Move your body, arms, weapons, shields, and held arrows farther away, closer, left, right, up, or down.[/*]
[*][b]Tune each equipment type:[/b] Set separate depth positions for melee weapons, bows, and magic, with one general position for everything else.[/*]
[*][b]Reduce body and weapon clipping:[/b] Apply automatic corrections during heavy attacks, sprint attacks, dodges, two-handed sheathing, and bonfire interactions.[/*]
[*][b]Advanced head-bob settings:[/b] Add smooth movement to your view without shaking your arms. Choose Subtle, Balanced, or Strong, then adjust smoothness and sprint emphasis.[/*]
[*][b]Keep everything visually connected:[/b] Multi-part weapons, held equipment, and supported visual effects remain aligned with your hands during movement and animation.[/*]
[*][b]Change presentation without changing gameplay:[/b] Adjustments apply immediately without affecting FOV, aiming, projectiles, hit detection, physics, or player movement.[/*]
[/list]

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Positioning[/size][/color][/heading]
Offsets are measured in meters relative to the camera:

[list]
[*]Positive [b]ForwardOffset[/b] moves the first-person model farther from the camera.[/*]
[*]Positive [b]HorizontalOffset[/b] moves it to the right.[/*]
[*]Positive [b]VerticalOffset[/b] moves it upward.[/*]
[/list]

Category offsets are enabled by default. Melee weapons and magic use 0.30, while bows use the smaller 0.10 offset. Start with forward values between 0.20 and 0.35. Set every offset to 0 or disable the plugin to restore the vanilla presentation.

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Head Bob[/size][/color][/heading]
The game's vanilla first-person head bob moves the same arm and body rig that FPAA repositions. With adjusted arms, this can pull shoulders and torso into the camera and cause severe clipping.

FPAA therefore suppresses the vanilla first-person bob and replaces it with custom movement applied only to your view. Your arms and body stay steady, while aiming, camera depth, and third person remain unchanged.

The game's [b]Accessibility / Head Bob[/b] toggle remains the global master switch. FPAA provides its own local toggle, Subtle/Balanced/Strong presets, adjustable smoothness, and sprint emphasis. Balanced strength, 0.7 smoothness, and 0.75 sprint emphasis are the defaults.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Rendering Scope And Limitations[/size][/color][/heading]
Head bob applies only a vertical and side-to-side translation while the main first-person camera renders, then restores the camera immediately. It is distance-driven, uses the Hero's sprint state, and never changes camera depth, rotation, aim, arms, or third person. Balanced with the default 0.75 Sprint Emphasis adds about 56% movement and 19% cadence during sprinting. Subtle lowers the movement ceiling; Strong raises it. Head Bob Smoothness adds up to 0.18 seconds of filtering. The off-by-default Suppress Motion Blur During Head Bob option excludes the bobbing camera's movement from HDRP motion blur on visible bob frames, preserves moving-object blur, and restores the exact prior value afterward.

Held one-handed, two-handed, dual-wielded, and alternate melee heavy attacks are handled specially: the complete offset eases toward the configured held-melee scale while the attack is raised or held, then applies the held-only forward and vertical corrections. The default scale retains the normal offset and pulls the held pose 0.05 metres toward the camera near plane and 0.05 metres down. One-handed and two-handed sprint attacks use the game's dedicated LightAttackForward state to return the complete presentation offset rapidly to vanilla, then restore it smoothly after the attack. Every directional dodge follows its animation toward vanilla and back to the configured offset before it ends. DodgeMoveTowardVanillaPercent controls that midpoint: 0 keeps the configured offset, the default 50 retains half, and 100 reaches vanilla. Chained and redirected dodges continue from the currently displayed offset instead of snapping outward mid-animation. When a melee weapon is sheathed in its active two-handed grip, the complete presentation offset eases to the vanilla position before the game hides the viewmodel, then restores safely after the transition or an interruption. Test melee weapons, bows, shields, magic, item use, swimming, and mounted gameplay when choosing offsets.

The plugin refreshes the active rig and equipment regions repeatedly, caches only the small active set of equipped presentation-effect roots, clears cached rig and render references during scene changes, and briefly suspends offsets while a replacement scene settles. Effects beneath the first-person body keep an adjusted emitter position between frames, then temporarily remove that child offset while the body-root render translation is active so local-space effects move exactly once. It changes presentation data only; the live animation skeleton and gameplay transforms remain untouched. AdjustAttachedEffects can disable the effect correction independently.

Bows use their normal category offset without a special full-draw hand correction. Released arrows originate from the unchanged gameplay fire point, so a small visual transition can remain when a held arrow becomes a projectile.

Because this mod adjusts native Kandra and Drake rendering data, treat unusually large offsets as experimental and test them across the activities you use.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Updating[/size][/color][/heading]
Incompatible config updates create a backup and generate current defaults. Some supported settings are automatically carried over.

FoA Mod Manager's final [b]Import Previous Settings[/b] tab shows the current and available backup schemas. Toggle the import action to restore compatible customized settings. Restart the game after importing.

Automatically preserved: Enabled, EnableHeadBob, HeadBobPreset, HeadBobSmoothness, SprintEmphasis, MitigateHeldMeleeBodyIntrusion, ForwardOffset, HorizontalOffset, VerticalOffset, MeleeForwardOffset, BowForwardOffset, MagicForwardOffset, HeldMeleeOffsetScale, HeldMeleeExtraForwardOffset, HeldMeleeExtraVerticalOffset, and Diagnostics.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Troubleshooting[/size][/color][/heading]
If arms or equipment appear clipped, hidden, or too distant, reduce the active category offset first. Set all offsets to 0 or disable [b]Enabled[/b] to confirm the vanilla presentation before testing another value.

Enable [b]Diagnostics[/b] to log head bob, the resolved first-person hierarchy, the Kandra rig, culling and linked Drake equipment paths receiving the offset, the cached equipped-effect count, and melee FSM state changes with independent held-heavy and sprint-attack mitigation results. Full details are written to [b]BepInEx/LogOutput.log[/b].

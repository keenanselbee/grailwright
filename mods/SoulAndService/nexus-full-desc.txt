[b]Soul and Service - Summon Overhaul[/b] transforms the vanilla Soul Salvage spell into [b]Soul Rend[/b], the heart of a complete necromancer playstyle. Defeat enemies, judge the value of their remains, and choose whether to harvest their souls or bind them into your growing host.

Soul Rend can harvest corpses, damage and claim living enemies, raise the fallen, carefully dismantle servants for Mana and Soul Vigor, and Empower the worthy. The souls you harvest build [b]Soul Vigor[/b], which becomes [b]Necromantic Power[/b] and transforms a novice summoner into the master of a larger, stronger, more disciplined army.

Your servants are no longer disposable bodies that wander, spin, block doorways, or disappear behind you. Order them to attack, hold ground, form around you, change their behavior, regroup from anywhere, and surge forward as a Swarm. Keep proven servants alive and their victories forge them into stronger, larger, brighter champions whose power can later be dismantled deliberately.

[heading][color=#38A840][size=5]Quick Start Guide[/size][/color][/heading]

[b][color=#ffffff]1. Harvest Soul Vigor[/color][/b]

Light-cast Soul Rend on almost any freshly killed non-summon corpse. Stronger corpses provide more Vigor, raising Necromantic Power and unlocking commands, capacity, and stronger servants. Protected bodies remain intact even after their soul is spent.

[b][color=#ffffff]2. Raise the Fallen[/color][/b]

Heavy-cast Soul Rend on a corpse to overcome its binding resistance. Failed attempts preserve progress, and Soul Vigor is spent only when binding succeeds. Ordinary summon spells also cost Vigor.

[b][color=#ffffff]3. Command the Host[/color][/b]

Use Sprint with Interact to Attack, Hold, Follow, or change behavior. Guard protects you, Bulwark forms a defensive screen, and Hunt aggressively seeks enemies. Sprint + Take All controls or Recalls the whole host.

[b][color=#ffffff]4. Sustain Your Servants[/color][/b]

At low Power, necromantic upkeep steadily drains servant Health. Heavy Soul Rend restores them; at Power 100, a healthy servant can be Empowered by spending twice its base Soul Vigor value at the current Power-scaled rate. From Power 40 onward, damage dealt also earns durable Soulforged ranks that remain until deliberately stripped.

[b][color=#ffffff]5. Spend and Rebuild[/color][/b]

Summoning and Empowering spend Soul Vigor and can lower Necromantic Power enough to relock abilities. Existing servants and completed Empower rituals are never undone, so harvest more souls to restore your mastery.

| Sprint + aim servant + tap Interact               | Hold / Follow                   |

[heading][color=#38A840][size=5]Soul Vigor Progression[/size][/color][/heading]

[b]Soul Vigor[/b] is earned by harvesting suitable corpses and servants. It becomes [b]Necromantic Power[/b] through a smooth mastery curve: Power 100 marks full normal mastery at 1,000 Soul Vigor, while a long overmastery path reaches Power 200 at 5,000.

Soul Vigor remains stored with the active character and can continue rising, but gameplay Power stops at 200. It is an integer resource rather than a permanent score: summoning and Empowering spend it, immediately lower Necromantic Power, and can relock commands or future Empower rituals until enough is harvested again. Existing servants and completed Empower rituals are not undone merely because a milestone relocks. Soul Vigor is separate from Blood Magic Expansion's Blood Essence even though both progressions share the same mastery curve.

[code]

|------------|-------|--------------------------------------------------|
| 0          | 0     | Reanimation and improved servant behavior        |
| 65         | 10    | Attack Command                                   |

These are command milestones, not sudden power jumps. Damage, resistance, raised health, binding strength, summon responsiveness, and upkeep improve smoothly between them.

[b]Summon Capacity[/b] lets mastery grow the host itself, adding +1 to the native summon limit at Power 50, +2 at Power 100, and +3 at Power 150. The configurable Summon Limit Bonus remains additional. Lowering the effective limit never destroys existing servants; it only prevents new summons until the host is below the current cap.

Damage and resistance apply to every hero-owned summon, including ordinary summons from native spells. These progression values are multiplied by the active unlocked behavior: Guard adds 5% damage and 5% mitigation, Bulwark adds 15% mitigation, and Hunt adds 10% damage plus 10% pursuit/combat speed. Raised starting health applies only to enemies brought back through Soul Rend.

[heading][color=#38A840][size=5]Soulforged Servants[/size][/color][/heading]

At Power 40, servants can begin earning permanent [b]Soulforged ranks[/b] through damage actually dealt to hostile enemies. Rank I requires damage equal to twice that servant's original maximum Health. A new rank opens every 10 Power, with later ranks asking more of the servant and the final Soulforged XVII available at Power 200 after 54 Health-bars of service.

Damage progress is never wasted. A servant that has fought enough but lacks the next Power milestone waits at 100% until your mastery catches up, and earned ranks never fall when Soul Vigor is spent. Light Soul Rend can deliberately remove two real ranks at a time, lowering stored damage to the new rank floor so those ranks must be earned again. Death, dismissal, or final unbinding still ends that servant's personal legend.

Each rank adds 1% damage, 1% matching resistance, 0.5% visible size, and its share of Reanimation VFX brightness. Visual rank growth multiplies with Empower under a firm 1.30x body-size ceiling, while combat scaling is unchanged. Simply aim at a servant to see its name, rank, Health, and progress toward the next ascent; rank changes announce themselves without adding another command input.

For testing, [b]Diagnostics > Override Soulforged Rank[/b] can temporarily force the exact effective rank of every current and future servant. Disabling it restores each servant's real saved progress.

[b]Attack[/b] unlocks at Power 10 and sends the eligible host after one visible enemy across the configured Targeting Range. The order remains authoritative until the target is lost, replaced, or cancelled by Recall. At Power 90 it becomes [b]Swarm[/b]: for five seconds servants close up to 1.25x faster, and each deals 1.25x damage on its first successful hit. Repeated orders refresh rather than stack the bonus.

A held servant guards its position within an 8-meter leash; an explicit Attack temporarily releases it, after which it returns. [b]Behavior control[/b] unlocks Guard and Hunt at Power 50, followed by Bulwark at Power 60. Hold Sprint + Interact over empty space for 0.45 seconds to cycle the available behaviors. The selected behavior is stored with the character, inherited by new servants, and immediately reevaluates the host without overriding an explicit Attack or Swarm order. If spent Vigor relocks Bulwark, the host safely falls back to Guard until it returns.

[heading][color=#38A840][size=5]Soul Rend Details[/size][/color][/heading]

Soul Rend changes with its target, turning every cast into a choice between immediate power, another body in the host, or the chance to claim a living foe.

[b]Light cast - Harvest or Rend[/b]

Almost every genuine non-summon corpse created during the current session can be harvested, regardless of faction or whether its NPC identity can be reused safely. Harvesting starts from native randomized Soul Vigor values of 2-4 Meager, 7-11 Worthy, 12-18 Potent, or 24-36 Prime before the active reward multiplier. Ordinary structurally safe bodies become one-copy, loot-preserving simplified remains. Unique, scripted, merchant, guard, companion, and otherwise protected bodies remain intact but become soul-spent: they cannot pay twice or be reanimated afterward. Corpses restored from an earlier session retain conservative structural eligibility, and summon corpses remain excluded to prevent renewable-Vigor farming.

Light casting an owned servant dismantles it in deliberate stages rather than destroying a veteran by accident. Empowerment is severed first for 75% of its exact recorded Vigor cost. Each later cast removes two real Soulforged ranks; every unique rank releases 3% of the original refundable Mana and base Soul Vigor at current Health with a 50% minimum efficiency. Re-earned ranks cannot pay twice. Only the next cast against an unempowered rank-0 servant completes unbinding and returns the remaining Health-scaled pool. A rank-XVII servant can expose 51% of its base pool before final unbinding without ever creating more principal.

With [url=https://www.nexusmods.com/taintedgrailthefallofavalon/mods/254]Eyes in the Dark[/url] 1.3.2 or newer installed, a successful ordinary corpse harvest reports its normalized quality through Eyes' soft API. Eyes applies its configured quality-scaled threat only during an exposed outdoor Wyrdnight. Failed and repeated harvests, greater-soul portions, living servant unbinding, and remains already executed through Blood Magic Expansion add no corpse-harvest threat.

Every summon also costs Soul Vigor. Before preset scaling, Power-100 tier-one through tier-six spells cost 3/6/9/12/15/18. Power 0 doubles those costs and Power 200 halves them, rounded up, while corpse reanimation follows the same curve against that corpse's stable soul value. Tooltips and interactions always show the effective price.

Against a living hostile, Soul Rend deals a normal hero-attributed magical hit at 50%/100%/200% of a comparable tier-one light spell at Power 0/100/200. Each hit the enemy survives raises that enemy's Soul Claim threshold by 2% for as long as it remains present, up to five hits and +10%. With Steel and Bone installed, the hit becomes Necrotic, making flesh, spirits, flora, and fungi vulnerable while corrupted, undead, skeletal, and constructed bodies increasingly resist it. Claim ignores those damage resistances, and living-target Soul Rend grants no Soul Vigor.

[b]Greater soul pools[/b]

Genuine saved scene-authored miniboss and boss corpses hold six equal, deterministic Soul Vigor portions. Runtime-spawned, summoned, respawned, temporary-death, kill-protected, and reward-free copies cannot create a pool. With Soul Rend equipped, the corpse's normal Search line shows only its remaining value, such as [b]54 Soul Vigor[/b]. Holding heavy cast replaces that line entirely with its reanimation price or a stable unbindable-spirit message.

| Native tier | Miniboss portion / total | Boss portion / total |

| 6           | 9 / 54                   | 11 / 66              |
| 7           | 10 / 60                  | 12 / 72              |[/code]

Each light cast removes and awards one portion. The final drain simplifies a structurally safe repetitive body through the normal one-copy, loot-preserving remains transaction. Protected unique, story, presence, dialogue, and merchant bodies stay intact at [b]0 Soul Vigor[/b] and can never be bound.

[b]Heavy cast - Bind or Claim[/b]

Heavy casting costs 30 base mana and challenges an eligible hostile corpse's binding resistance. Failed attempts save progress across reloads, while higher Power adds more progress per cast and Power 200 binds any eligible corpse in one. A valid hover previews [b]Reanimate: X Soul Vigor[/b] or [b]Requires X Soul Vigor[/b]. The cost is committed only after binding succeeds.

If the crosshair rests on nearby ground or harmless clutter, heavy Soul Rend can assist to an eligible corpse within 0.4 meters without selecting through walls or floors.

Living enemies can instead be [b]Claimed[/b] deterministically when their Health reaches the final threshold. Base thresholds are 5%/10%/15%/20%/25%/30% at Power 25/50/75/100/150/200. Meager, Worthy, Potent, and Prime souls resist by 0%/3%/6%/9%; every surviving light hit adds 2% up to +10%; and the active preset adds -5%/+0%/+5%. The result is clamped to 1-40%.

Success executes the enemy, immediately raises it through the normal protected corpse lifecycle, and gives the new servant the same starting Health it would receive from ordinary corpse reanimation.

Failure leaves the enemy alive. Success awards the normal kill and XP once, then raises a reward-free servant through the protected reanimation lifecycle. Claim grants no immediate Soul Vigor, although the resulting servant can later be harvested for its corpse-quality reward.

[heading][color=#38A840][size=5]Responsive Servants[/size][/color][/heading]

Soul and Service improves hero-owned summons only; ordinary NPC allies remain unchanged. Servants retain the game's native faction, ownership, combat logic, status effects, and damage types, while Summon Capacity builds on the native summon limit.

[b]Following[/b]

Idle servants trot when 4 meters behind, run at 8, and safely teleport at 60, with 1.25x catch-up speed outside combat. Guard, Hunt, Bulwark, Recall, and directed movement use stable, size-aware positions that keep servants separated, close empty ranks, accommodate large and Empowered bodies, and recover from blocked destinations without persistent spinning or crowding.

Guard, Close Guard, and Hunt ignore brief player movements, follow smoothly once movement becomes deliberate, and settle shortly after the hero stops. Bulwark Advance responds immediately while Sprint is held. Automatic catch-up teleports use the green-dark summoning effect, while native scene-transition relocation remains silent. Idle Movement Amount controls how often settled Guard and Hunt servants wander near their positions; zero disables voluntary wandering without disabling formation correction.

[b]Persistent Servants[/b] is enabled by default, keeping ordinary summons plus each raised servant's source identity, Health, Empowerment, investment, Soulforged progress, and finite staged-recovery ledger through saves, reloads, and game restarts. Generated raised copies never enter the native save graph. If persistence is disabled or one servant cannot be reconstructed safely, its protected source is restored from the same all-or-nothing recovery snapshot instead.

Ordinary summons remain on the game's native save path. Soul Rend-raised copies are instead rebuilt from one versioned host snapshot after the main scene is ready, keeping their Health, Empowerment, Soulforged progress, investment, and protected source relationship without placing the generated NPC tree in the native save graph. If a raised record cannot be trusted or reconstructed safely, its source is restored and trustworthy committed Soul Vigor is refunded.

Ordinary summons, fresh raised servants, persistently restored servants, and replacement servants are genuine hero summons, so Soul and Service keeps the game's nine Summoning talents native:

Failed reconstruction, invalid-record recovery, source restoration, plugin shutdown, and other administrative cleanup discard the generated copy without fabricating a death trigger.

[b]Awareness[/b]

Guard and Hunt consider faction hostility and line of sight from either the hero or acting servant, while Bulwark stays inside its short defensive zone. Steel and Bone contributes 80% of its added sight range and pursuit persistence within the game's native 45-meter summon leash. Explicit commands always take priority.

Optional passive crosshair sharing lets an uncommitted servant adopt the hostile under the hero's crosshair. It is disabled by default and remains governed by the selected behavior.

[b]Collision and native combat[/b]

[b]Summon Pass-Through[/b] lets the hero move through servants while they continue colliding with enemies, the world, and incoming attacks. The default [b]CombatOnly[/b] Player Attack Pass-Through mode also lets confirmed hero projectiles and magic-gauntlet contacts pass through servants during combat; Vanilla, MagicOnly, and AllProjectiles modes are available.

Idle-loop volume defaults to 60%; combat sounds remain unchanged.

[heading][color=#38A840][size=5]Raised Servants, Upkeep, and Safety[/size][/color][/heading]

[b]Safe reanimation[/b]

Heavy Soul Rend accepts ordinary hostile corpses and living enemies that can safely become native hero summons. Named or scripted characters, quest actors, merchants, guards, companions, bosses, unallowlisted minibosses, friendlies, claimed corpses, blocked targets, and enemies without safe summon data are rejected.

Those strict rules apply only to binding. A fresh protected corpse may still yield Soul Vigor while its body remains intact. Heavy Soul Rend reports [b]This soul is too resistant to bind[/b] for an intact corpse that cannot safely become a servant, or [b]No soul remains to bind[/b] after that corpse has already been harvested.

Successful binding protects the original corpse and creates an allied copy. When that servant dies, is dismissed, or is harvested, its original loot appears in simplified remains at its last safe position, with the corpse location as a fallback. Failed initialization safely restores the protected source instead. Persistent hosts reconnect the servant to that same protected corpse after loading rather than duplicating rewards.

Persistent reanimation additionally requires one canonical reusable NPC spawn template. Ambiguous or runtime-only spawn data is rejected before Soul Vigor is committed.

[b]Miniboss service[/b]

Selected minibosses have no Power gate, but must pass a stricter identity check: the exact non-scalable NPC template must be allowlisted, and that individual corpse must be a saved, full, repetitive, nonunique, unprotected scene body backed by one durable LocationTemplate. The initial allowlist covers Foredweller Weaver and Weaver Mage, Blood Abomination, Rootambusher, Forlorn Ogre, and Sleepwalker templates. An encounter using one of those families is still rejected if any typed safety condition fails. Bosses, scalable Drowned Knights, Tibby, and the Lancelot-crypt Eldritch Reaver remain unbindable.

Binding has 340 base resistance, twice Prime, and each service cycle begins a fresh deterministic attempt ledger. The base reanimation price is 120 Soul Vigor through the ordinary Power curve, rising by 10% after each completed service. At Power 100, the six prices are 120/132/144/156/168/180. The soul pool is not spent when service begins: combat death, upkeep, rest, dismissal, execution, or light unbinding consumes one portion without awarding it, while failed initialization, saving, loading, scene travel, and Recall do not.

A miniboss occupies two Summon Capacity slots and only one may serve at once. It begins with a full bar at 50% native maximum Health, deals 60% damage before ordinary Power, behavior, and Soulforged scaling, receives half normal Soul Rend healing, and cannot be Claimed, Raised All, Empowered, Blood-drained, or harvested for refunds. Its Soulforged progress lasts for that service only.

The original full corpse and its native Search inventory remain the only saved inventory owner. Before service, that same corpse is promoted to the game's native saved runtime form while preserving any authored prefab override, its authored scene ID is permanently retired, and it moves hidden into Gameplay; the reward-free combat copy owns no loot. When service ends, the full corpse returns to the current scene at the servant's last safe position and stays there across saves until it is bound or drained again. Deferred recovery keeps it hidden until a main scene is ready and its runtime source identity validates. The retired scene ID cannot spawn another copy. On the sixth service end, only that canonical source becomes normal simplified remains with native empty-container cleanup. If terminal conversion fails, the last portion is restored and the full corpse remains available for retry.

[b]Necromantic upkeep[/b]

Necromantic weakness makes an early host fiercely temporary. At full Health, three servants last about 3 minutes at Power 0, 20 minutes at Power 50, and 50 minutes at Power 90 before upkeep alone kills them. One servant receives half that drain and seven or more receive twice that drain, with intermediate host sizes scaling smoothly. Ordinary-servant upkeep continues during combat, can be lethal, and ends only at Power 100. Heavy Soul Rend restoration is therefore essential for sustaining an ambitious host before full mastery.

A miniboss counts as one body for host-size pressure, but its own active and rest upkeep is twice the ordinary host rate through Power 90. From that Power-90 value it fades slowly to zero only at Power 200, so sustaining a greater soul remains meaningful throughout overmastery without doubling upkeep on ordinary servants.

[b]Rest and persistence[/b]

The default [b]Sustain[/b] rest behavior keeps the host but makes long rests costly. An eight-hour rest at Power 0 removes 45% maximum Health from one servant, with each additional servant adding 18% to the whole host up to a 90% cap. Shorter rests scale by their actual duration, Power steadily reduces the loss, and Power 100 removes it entirely. The rest screen previews only nonzero loss that applies to the current host, warns when servants may perish, preserves the native Wyrdnight warning, and composes cleanly with Glorious UI. Choose [b]Dismiss[/b] instead to end the host through its normal safe remains lifecycle.

[b]Restore and Empower[/b]

Heavy Soul Rend restores one friendly servant by 20%/35%/50% maximum Health at Power 0/100/200. Below 95% Health, restoration is the cast's only service even when it fills the Health bar. At or above 95%, Soul Rend generously restores the remainder and, from Power 100 (1,000 Soul Vigor), can [b]Empower[/b] the servant in the same ritual.

Empower costs twice the servant's stable base Soul Vigor value, reduced by the same Power curve as reanimation. The hover shows [b]Empower: X Soul Vigor[/b] or [b]Requires X Soul Vigor[/b], and payment occurs only when Empower succeeds. Its exact investment is recorded separately; light Soul Rend must sever Empowerment before touching ranks and immediately returns 75% of that cost. A later new Empower ritual records its own new payment.

A lower-biased 1.2x-1.5x combat roll improves both damage and resistance, while visible Empower growth scales from 1.05x-1.20x. Soulforged contributes another 0.5% visible size per rank, and the combined result never exceeds 1.30x. Empower cannot stack while active, but Empowered servants can still be restored.

[b]Necromantic presentation[/b]

Reanimation and Recall use the Forgotten Cemetery necromancer's green-dark summoning effect. Raised servants carry configurable dark-green arcs, a corpse-green core, and deep body-bound smoke; ordinary summons gain the treatment when they become Soulforged. Rank and Empowerment interpolate from the default 5 [b]Base Brightness[/b] to 20 [b]Full Potential Brightness[/b] while drawing all three colors toward saturated emerald by default. [b]Use Custom Full Potential Color[/b] can instead blend them toward a configurable endpoint, including the original white presentation.

Every servant retains its native NPC portrait, with the vanilla skeleton-summon portrait as a fallback, so the summon HUD always has a valid image. Soul Rend hands emit a configurable no-shadow green light. Forty quality-matched ritual sounds give stronger souls greater weight, while separate light and heavy impact sounds make every valid Soul Rend contact feel immediate. Every custom clip follows the game's SFX mixer, including its Master and SFX volume controls and mute states.

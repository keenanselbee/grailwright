## Executive Summary

Requiem’s stated goal is **not** “make Skyrim enemies into HP sponges” or “just crank the difficulty slider.” Its own Nexus description explicitly draws a line between **harder combat** and a **more dangerous, more logical world**. In Requiem, danger comes from readable asymmetry: low protection means arrows and blades can kill you quickly; heavy protection answers arrows but becomes vulnerable to magic and mobility problems; specific enemy types demand specific tools; and the world is deleveled so threat is tied to the encounter, not to the player’s level scaling. Requiem says this directly: it wants a world that is “more dangerous,” where progress comes from growth, preparation, and adapting in real time. citeturn26view0turn26view1turn26view5

That difference matters for *Steel and Bone*. “Harder combat” is when every enemy simply hits harder and takes longer to kill. “More dangerous, more legible combat” is when the player can usually explain **why** an encounter felt bad or good: *I used the wrong damage type; I brought pierce into bone; I tried bleed on a construct; I stood at the wrong range against a ranged elite; I failed to switch when the target’s family became obvious*. Requiem’s best lesson is not its total-conversion scale. It is its insistence that **enemy identity, gear identity, and build identity must be visible through combat outcomes**. citeturn26view0turn24view2turn14view5

Official Requiem sources confirm a large part of this comes from broad Skyrim-wide edits: a deleveled world, static enemy levels, perk-centric builds, weapon damage-type keywords, armor-specific resistances, custom ammo penetration tiers, mass-and-armor scripts, leveled-list edits, and a preprocessing patcher called the Reqtificator. Those are powerful, but they are **not** the right blueprint for a narrow BepInEx mod in *Tainted Grail*. citeturn26view5turn16view1turn14view5turn11view0turn12view1turn13view0

The correct translation for *Steel and Bone* is much smaller: keep your current family classification approach, expand it into a tighter resistance/weakness table, make a few high-signal matchup rules stronger, expose those rules with readable feedback, and avoid flat global punishment that makes combat harsher without increasing tactical decision-making. If the player starts asking, **“What is this enemy, what damage am I using, and should I switch?”**, then you have captured the useful part of Requiem without copying its total-conversion baggage. That is exactly the kind of tactical adaptation Requiem says it is chasing, and exactly the scale that fits your current mod architecture. citeturn26view2turn26view4turn26view6

## Evidence Basis and What Requiem Actually Does

For this report, I treated **official Requiem sources** as the ground truth for confirmed behavior: the Nexus description, the official GitHub wiki, the changelog, and the official documentation pages for artifacts and modding keywords. These sources confirm Requiem’s goals, its broad Skyrim-wide scope, its armor/damage-type framework, its custom penetration and resistance keywording, and many specific matchup rules such as shock doing extra work into constructs, silver or sun effects against undead, and deleveling/static-level encounter logic. citeturn3view0turn10view0turn6view0turn23view0turn13view0turn13view2

I used **community discussions** only for two narrower purposes: first, to capture how players *interpret* Requiem’s rock-paper-scissors combat in practice; second, to identify where that design becomes frustrating, overprescriptive, or too dependent on meta-knowledge. Community threads consistently summarize the archetype triangle as **archers or rogues pressuring mages, mages pressuring heavily armored melee, and armored melee answering arrows and light targets**. They also highlight practical play patterns such as using cover, shields, blur, and matchup-specific gear instead of trying to stat-check every encounter. citeturn18view1turn18view2turn18view3turn18view0

Where I recommend specific *Steel and Bone* multipliers, family rules, or staged roadmap items, that part is **design inference**, not a claim that Requiem already uses those exact numbers. I am translating Requiem’s directional logic into a smaller BepInEx-friendly ruleset because Requiem’s own official documentation makes clear that its full behavior depends on game-wide record edits, custom patching, and intertwined systems that would be disproportionate for your stated scope. citeturn26view5turn13view0turn11view0turn12view1

One useful caution from the community is worth retaining even if you like Requiem: some players praise its “logical” encounter identity, while others criticize its spikes, meta-knowledge demands, and the way certain counters can become too mandatory. That split is relevant to *Steel and Bone* because your mod should borrow **clarity and matchup identity**, not **opaque gotchas** or “bring the one correct answer or lose” design. citeturn18view4turn21view0turn19view9

## Requiem Combat Model

Requiem’s official description gives the cleanest high-level map: **warriors in armor answer arrows**, **mages answer armored warriors**, and **rogues or rangers answer exposed mages**. Official systems under that umbrella include damage-type keywords on weapons, armor resistances on cuirasses, ammunition penetration tiers, material bonuses, spell-cost penalties in armor, and large amounts of enemy-specific protection or vulnerability work. Community discussions then fill in how players *experience* those systems in live play. citeturn26view4turn24view2turn14view5turn18view1turn18view3

The most important takeaway here is that Requiem’s combat triangle is **not** just “class A beats class B.” It becomes interesting because it branches into **enemy families**, **damage subtypes**, **ammo quality**, **armor material**, **gear preparation**, and **specific elite exceptions**. ## Actionable Steel and Bone Ideas

The core design target should be simple: every common enemy family should answer **one or two damage types well**, suffer **one or two damage types clearly**, and communicate that fact in a way the player can learn after a single fight. That is the smallest version of Requiem’s combat philosophy that still feels tactical. Requiem’s own documentation and community play reports support this style of design: danger is high, but success comes from reading the encounter rather than from hoping your flat DPS wins anyway. citeturn26view1turn24view2turn18view3

For lethal combat to remain fair in a game where the player usually chooses engagement range, opening attack, and retreat timing, the rules should follow five constraints. First, the target family must be identifiable before or immediately after first contact. Second, common-family weaknesses should be large enough to notice but not so large that the first correct weapon always erases the fight. Third, elites should use the same rules as normal enemies with narrower weakness windows, not invisible boss exemptions. Fourth, anti-cheese measures should trigger only on repeat abuse, not on legitimate scouting or disengage. Fifth, feedback should tell the player what happened instead of asking them to datamine it. These are *Steel and Bone* design recommendations, but they align with Requiem’s official emphasis on tactical adaptation and its community-described use of cover, counterplay, and preparation. citeturn26view1turn18view1turn18view3

## Design Warnings and Final Recommendation

### What not to copy from Requiem

Do **not** copy Requiem’s total-conversion dependency chain. Official sources repeatedly show that Requiem works because it changes nearly everything: combat, magic, player stats, races, perks, leveled lists, encounters, itemization, artifacts, and much of the world’s progression logic. It requires a new game, uses a single intertwined plugin, delevels the game world, keeps actors static-level, and relies on the Reqtificator plus custom keywords and delta-patch workflows for compatibility. That is not “a few matchup rules.” It is a whole alternate Skyrim. citeturn26view5turn16view1turn11view0turn12view1turn13view0turn13view2

Do **not** copy Requiem’s broad stamina and mass systems unless your hook surface makes one or two specific features trivial to implement. Requiem’s mass effect stores armor penalties in actor values and calculates them through a dedicated script. It also layers movement-speed changes, jump stamina, spell-cost penalties in armor, perk gating, and additional combat interactions around that foundation. In *Steel and Bone*, trying to mimic that through partial hooks would likely produce a brittle system that is harder to understand than a clean damage table. citeturn22view3turn22view4turn22view7

Do **not** copy Requiem’s need for content-wide encounter patching. The official wiki states that weapons, armor, encounters, magic mods, leveled lists, and many gameplay systems need compatibility patching because Requiem delevels the world and makes perks and static actor setup central to balance. That is the exact kind of burden your current mod philosophy says you want to avoid. citeturn13view0turn16view1

Do **not** let “family identity” become “single mandatory answer.” Community praise for Requiem focuses on having counters, multiple options, cover use, and preparation. Community criticism appears where the design becomes too spiky, too opaque, or too meta-dependent. For *Steel and Bone*, that means most special families should have one strong answer and one acceptable backup, not one exclusive solution. citeturn18view1turn18view3turn21view0turn19view9

Do **not** solve lethality with flat damage multiplication alone. Requiem’s own description explicitly rejects the idea that its value comes from enemies merely dealing more damage and being harder to kill. If *Steel and Bone* just raises damage numbers globally, players will die faster, but they will not learn faster. Tactical combat needs *readable causality*. citeturn26view0turn26view1

### Final recommendation

If I were prioritizing *Steel and Bone* for maximum tactical gain per implementation hour, I would ship these **twelve changes first**:

| Priority change | Why it is worth doing first |
|---|---|
| **Bone resists Slash and Pierce, fears Blunt** | This is the cleanest, most legible material rule you can add, and it immediately teaches weapon identity. |
| **Confirmed no-bleed/no-poison targets reject body status** | It makes enemy family visible without falsely treating every undead template as identical. |
| **Constructs ignore Bleed and Poison, resist Blood** | A very cheap rule with huge clarity. Machines stop behaving like meat. |
| **Construct exceptions preserve Electric where confirmed** | This keeps the Requiem conduction lesson while respecting TG's per-template construct polarities. |
| **Animated Armor fears Blunt more than Slash** | Gives armor enemies a reason to feel distinct from bone enemies without extra systems. |
| **Beasts and Flesh enemies reward Bleed and Poison** | Status builds get a real home instead of feeling like bad universal tools. |
| **Wyrd rules stay explicit and tested** | Gives Wyrd its own lane without pretending the templates prove a blanket Wyrdness weakness or resistance. |
| **Generic Physical becomes fallback, not best answer** | Preserves the value of your current damage taxonomy. |
| **Family tag readout in names or combat text** | If players can see the family, they can actually play the system. |
| **Combat reason text with rate limiting** | Tells the player *why* damage changed, which is essential for fairness. |
| **Preset profiles that preserve matchup shape** | Lets users choose severity without breaking legibility. |
| **Elite weakness clamp** | Keeps bosses dangerous without needing hidden immunities or unfair stat walls. |

If you want a short decision rule for future additions, use this one: **only add a rule if it makes an encounter ask a better question than “do I have enough DPS?”** Requiem’s best combat ideas do that constantly. Its worst, or at least most controversial, ideas are the ones that demand whole-game infrastructure or too much meta-knowledge. *Steel and Bone* should copy the first category and reject the second. citeturn26view1turn18view4turn21view0turn19view9

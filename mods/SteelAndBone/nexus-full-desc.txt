[center][color=#C94A4A][size=6]Steel and Bone[/size][/color]
[i]Bone, flesh, stone, and spirit. Know your enemy. Strike with purpose.[/i][/center]

[line]

[b]Steel and Bone[/b] is a lightweight, native-first difficulty overhaul built around one idea: knowing your enemy should matter.

Its material system gives bone, flesh, stone, spirit, armor, and other identities distinct reactions to physical, elemental, and status damage. Tainted Grail's existing weaknesses, resistances, and immunities remain authoritative; Steel and Bone adds focused rules only where they sharpen the matchup.

The result is a modular difficulty layer that rewards preparation and understanding without replacing enemy AI, rewriting encounters, or inflating health bars.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Combat Philosophy[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone follows one central rule: no combat style should answer every opponent. Arrows are deadly against exposed flesh and lightly protected casters, elemental magic turns armor's material against it through effects such as heated plate and electrified metal, and heavy armor and shields resist archery.

This triangle is the clearest expression of a wider material system rather than a rigid class restriction. Blunt weapons shatter bone and rigid constructs, blades and fire tear through flora, electricity shocks drowned and sea-dwelling enemies, and spirits resist ordinary weapons and biological effects. Good matchups provide a meaningful advantage, but player skill, equipment, and build choices still matter.

This philosophy is inspired by [url=https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/60888]Requiem - The Roleplaying Overhaul's[/url] lethal emphasis on coherent rules, preparation, and intelligent tactical play. Steel and Bone translates those ideas into Tainted Grail's native combat systems; it is not an attempt to reproduce the scope of a total conversion. It aims to be lightweight and modular above all.

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Know Your Enemy[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone classifies enemies through reachable runtime and template evidence before using editable name terms as fallback. Stronger and more specific families take precedence over broad categories, and elite enemies soften custom extremes without losing their identity.

[code]
| Enemy family      | Better answers                  | Poor answers                               |
|-------------------|---------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Bone bodies       | Blunt                           | Slash, pierce, arrows                      |
| Bone undead       | Blunt                           | Blood, bleed, Cold, slash, pierce, arrows  |
| Stone bodies      | Blunt; exact elemental counters | Edges, arrows                              |
| Constructs        | Blunt; exact elemental counters | Blood, bleed, poison, Cold, edges, arrows  |
| Armored humanoids | Blunt, spells                   | Slash, untyped physical, arrows vs Heavy   |
| Ordinary flesh    | Blood, poison, edges            | No broad resistance                        |
| Flesh undead      | Fire, blunt                     | Blood, bleed, poison, pierce, arrows       |
| Wyrd creatures    | Cold vs Slimes; slash vs spawn  | No broad resistance; native reactions vary |
| Drowned corpses   | Electric, blunt                 | Blood, bleed, pierce, arrows               |
| Infected flesh    | Fire, slash, arrows             | Poison                                     |
| Sea creatures     | Electric, slash, arrows         | Cold                                       |
| Spirits           | Wyrdness                        | Biological and ordinary physical attacks   |
| Flora and fungus  | Fire, slash, axes               | Poison, bleed, pierce, arrows              |
| Swarms            | Area and non-arrow attacks      | Individual arrow impacts                   |[/code]

Exact strength depends on the preset and the enemy's native data. Existing weaknesses and resistances are preserved or strengthened rather than duplicated, and true immunities remain intact.

Body material and creature identity are resolved independently. Stagfather and the Straw spirits retain bone-body weapon reactions without becoming Bone Undead, while Sleepwalker retains stone-body reactions as a Wyrd creature without inheriting Construct biological resistance. Ghost of Broc Meala is a Spirit, Wights use their Wyrd-flora identity, Wailcaps remain Sea Creatures, and Wyrd Slimes no longer inherit ordinary flesh weaknesses. Wyrd creatures have no blanket Wyrdness resistance; authored native and exact reactions still win.

Bone Undead and Constructs resist Cold at x0.66 on Hardened where their native data is neutral. This changes Cold damage and impact rather than independent spell effects, so spells that apply Chill can still slow them unless the target has a separate status immunity. Cold retains focused positive matchups against Flamegobblers, crystal bodies, Wyrd Slimes, and the Charred Conclave Wyrdspawn, and completes missing reactions on Grindylow, Blood Abomination, and Bonemask Warrior summons. Curlghasts, Marrowghasts, Slugholders, and Snail remain neutral unless their native data says otherwise. Frostgrot, Frostbitten Warriors, Frost Wolves, Ice Trial Wyrd creatures, and other exact frost-aligned enemies instead resist Cold, while Fire provides their natural counter. Wet answers explicitly fire-aligned bodies and golems, and Electric gains a modest lane against drowned skeleton Deckhands and Mariners without weakening Bone Undead broadly. Singworms and Lir's summoned tentacle favor Slash over Blunt; Blood Abominations favor Slash and resist Blunt, while Wyrd Slimes and Tidewraiths absorb Blunt impact through exact soft-body profiles. These remain exact archetype rules rather than universal weaknesses for Wyrd creatures, flora, or animals.

When a physical hit lacks a clear subtype, weapon identity fills the gap: swords and axes lean Slashing, daggers, polearms, and bows lean Piercing, and blunt weapons remain Bludgeoning. Axes receive x1.20 against wood and flora on Hardened, replacing rather than stacking with the ordinary flora Slash rule.

[b]Humanoid armor[/b]

Equipped armor creates a consistent physical matchup curve on Hardened:

[code]
| Attack type      | Exposed | Light | Medium | Heavy |
|------------------|---------|-------|--------|-------|
| Arrows           | x1.20   | x1.08 | x1.00  | x0.75 |
| Pierce           | x1.06   | x1.03 | x1.00  | x0.90 |
| Slash            | x1.04   | x0.98 | x0.92  | x0.82 |
| Blunt            | x1.00   | x1.00 | x1.08  | x1.15 |
| Generic physical | x1.00   | x0.98 | x0.94  | x0.88 |[/code]

Edges lose effectiveness as armor becomes heavier, while blunt weapons become stronger answers. When native armor is already reducing a hit, Steel and Bone softens its added resistance so the two systems do not compound into impractical damage.


[b]Arrows and spells[/b]

Direct player arrows use their own material matchup instead of being treated as generic Pierce. On Hardened, favorable flesh receives x1.10 to x1.15 physical arrow damage, while resistant targets range from x0.85 for flesh undead and drowned enemies to x0.20 for confirmed skeletons. Flora, spirits, constructs, and swarms fall between those extremes. Only the physical share changes, so Fire, Electric, and other payloads keep their own reactions.

Magic follows material identity rather than ignoring armor. On Hardened, direct spells receive x1.02 / x1.07 / x1.12 against Light, Medium, and Heavy armor. Fire remains useful against Fabric, Leather, and Metal; Electricity is strongest against Metal; and Cold gains a smaller edge against Metal. Blood, Wyrdness, biological effects, and spells that already bypass armor receive no duplicate bonus. Native reactions remain authoritative.

Material impact keeps reactions proportional to effectiveness. Every resisted direct player hit carries 60% of its resistance into reduced poise and force, without letting weaknesses amplify control. Immune or very strongly resisted hits also lose the routine small flinch. Genuine poise breaks, force stumbles, ragdolls, damage processing, effects, and aggro remain intact.

[b]A smoother late-game difficulty curve[/b]

Tainted Grail can be at its toughest early on, only for powerful builds to make later fights end too quickly. Progressive Tenacity is designed to smooth out that difficulty curve without making the opening hours harder.

It begins gradually after level 20. Regular enemies receive only a small benefit, while Elites, MiniBosses, and Bosses become better at standing their ground against burst damage, repeated staggers, and stamina pressure.

Enemies do not gain enormous health bars or immunity to your abilities. Good equipment and strong builds still matter, and exploiting a material weakness cuts through much of an enemy's Tenacity. The goal is simply to keep late-game combat engaging for longer.

Tenacity works with every difficulty preset, leaves the early game unchanged, and can be disabled with [b]ProgressiveTenacityEnabled[/b].

A compact technique layer gives focused builds a fallback without replacing the best material counter. Pommel strikes borrow the Blunt matchup against bone, stone, and ordinary armor; heavy melee attacks partially breach custom rigid resistance; and direct area hits pressure otherwise-neutral swarms. Native reactions still take priority.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Readable Combat Feedback[/size][/color][/heading]


[b]Floating damage numbers[/b]

Built-in floating damage numbers make matchup rules readable at a glance:

[list]
[*]Resisted hits become smaller and greyer; weaknesses become larger and warmer.[/*]
[*]Immunities use dedicated text and styling.[/*]
[*]Critical and weak-spot hits gain a red precision tint, softened when the target resists the underlying damage.[/*]
[*]Bleed, Poison, Burn, and Breath ticks appear higher and smaller so repeated effects remain legible.[/*]
[/list]


[b]Effectiveness Feedback Sensitivity[/b] controls how strongly resistance and weakness are presented without changing damage. Confirmed weak spots add 10%, 20%, or 30% base damage according to the preset, while native critical damage remains unchanged.

Number size, color, font, duration, drift, damage-over-time presentation, and maximum active numbers remain configurable.


[b]Stamina Depleted presentation[/b]

Smooth is the default and replaces the native repeating vignette flash plus abrupt disappearance with one eased fade in and fade out using the game's existing image. Native restores the original presentation, while Off hides both the HUD vignette and stamina-depleted post-process. The default 0.30-second Smooth transition is configurable; every mode preserves native exhaustion audio, movement penalties, action restrictions, and status behavior.


[b]Optional crosshair hit markers[/b]

[url=https://www.nexusmods.com/taintedgrailthefallofavalon/mods/223]Dishonored Dynamic Crosshair[/url] 3.0.0 or newer can present the same final result at the center of the screen through eight states ranging from immunity and resistance through neutral damage to weakness.

Independent weak-spot, critical, damage-over-time, and killing-blow corpse-quality overlays remain supported. Hit markers work without floating damage numbers and update immediately with each hit.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Expanded Combat Systems[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone increases combat pressure without replacing Tainted Grail's native AI or encounter design. Enemies become more alert and coordinated, projectiles become more dangerous, and armor choices gain clearer roles. Each major system remains independently configurable.


[b]Enemy awareness and pressure[/b]

Enemies see and hear farther, retain combat attention longer, and coordinate more simultaneous attacks as the preset rises. Attack opportunities also return sooner, making groups less likely to wait passively while one enemy fights alone.

Hardened and Crucible give ordinary agile enemies a modest combat movement bonus. Heavy enemies, bosses, constructs, plants, bears, scripted creatures, and non-pathing actors retain their native speed. Line of sight, walls, chase boundaries, and individual enemy behavior continue to work normally.


[b]Projectile combat[/b]

Player and hostile arrows travel at x1.10 / x1.30 / x1.50 speed across Tempered, Hardened, and Crucible. Player arrows use x0.75 gravity on every preset and fly flatter, while hostile archers retain 1.50 / 1.25 / 1.00 meters of aim scatter. Archers become more immediate threats without gaining unnatural accuracy.

Native aiming, movement prediction, collision, draw strength, animations, and elemental payloads remain intact. Direct player arrows also receive Steel and Bone's material matchups, but only their physical damage is adjusted.


[b]Armor roles[/b]

Armor gains clearer strengths and tradeoffs:

[list]
[*][b]Light armor[/b] supports mobility and gains a small movement bonus on higher presets.[/*]
[*][b]Medium armor[/b] provides improved physical protection without the full burden of Heavy armor.[/*]
[*][b]Heavy armor[/b] provides the strongest physical protection but carries greater movement and resource costs.[/*]
[/list]

Native armor proficiency, weight classes, individual penalties, and Overload behavior remain authoritative. Magical armor checks are unchanged.


[b]Passive shield protection[/b]

A readied shield provides modest protection against direct physical attacks from the front, even when it is not actively raised. Protection scales with the shield's native Block rating and reaches up to 8% / 10% / 12% on Tempered, Hardened, and Crucible.

Active blocking remains fully native and never receives the passive reduction twice. Hidden shields, rear attacks, magic, status effects, damage over time, falls, traps, and environmental damage receive no passive protection.


[b]Player combat pressure[/b]

Across the presets, incoming damage rises while outgoing damage falls. Higher presets also increase stamina and mana costs and reduce repeated stagger pressure, making defense and resource management more important.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Preparation and Recovery[/size][/color][/heading]

Food provides sustained recovery before and between fights, while Potion Poisoning discourages repeatedly drinking the same type of potion under pressure. Together, these systems reward preparation without replacing Tainted Grail's native consumables.


[b]Food recovery[/b]

Food becomes sustained preparation instead of a rapid combat heal. Its native health effect remains in place, preserving the faded queued-healing display on the health bar, while Steel and Bone extends recovery over a longer period.

[list]
[*]Every preset extends standard food to four times its original duration.[/*]
[*]Tempered restores health at x0.50 rate for 200% total healing, Hardened at x0.375 for 150%, and Crucible at x0.25 for the original total.[/*]
[*]Qualifying food also restores exactly 1 stamina each second for the same duration, including during ordinary regeneration lockouts from actions.[/*]
[*]If you run out of stamina while food is active, its stamina recovery pauses, but Overexertion and Stamina Depleted last only half as long. Food stamina recovery resumes immediately when Overexertion ends, while health recovery and the food timer continue throughout.[/*]
[*]Selecting Tempered allows combat food use, while Hardened and Crucible prevent it. The setting remains freely customizable afterward, and noncombat food use is always unrestricted. Blocked attempts are silent unless Grail Floating Text is installed, which shows a brief food-styled explanation.[/*]
[*]Food effects cannot stack. Eating again keeps whichever active meal offers the greatest remaining health recovery, with remaining duration breaking a tie.[/*]
[*]Health and stamina share one status, timer, and icon. Item descriptions update without adding Steel and Bone or preset labels.[/*]
[/list]


[b]Potion Poisoning[/b]

Repeatedly drinking the same class of potion builds toward Potion Poisoning. Health, Mana, Stamina, and Utility potions each use their own buildup, so mixing different classes is safer than repeatedly relying on one resource.

[list]
[*]Repeating one class within 2 seconds on Tempered, 3 seconds on Hardened, or 4 seconds on Crucible triggers poisoning.[/*]
[*]Health, Mana, and Stamina poisoning drain 30% of the corresponding maximum resource over the status duration.[/*]
[*]Utility poisoning drains 15% of maximum Health, Mana, and Stamina together. Buffs, cures, locks, regeneration potions, resets, and miscellaneous consumables share this class.[/*]
[*]A potion that restores more than one resource counts toward each matching class but can produce only one poisoning status.[/*]
[*]Triggering any class clears all four buildup pools. New buildup pauses while Potion Poisoning remains active.[/*]
[*]Normal healing and regeneration remain active and can offset the drain. Health poisoning is nonlethal and stops at 1 HP; Mana and Stamina can fall to zero.[/*]
[*]Potion restoration, unrelated effects, item text, and Better UI presentation otherwise remain native.[/*]
[/list]

Potion Poisoning uses the game's native status icon and decay. Its purpose is to discourage rapid repetition, not to prevent careful or varied potion use.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Presets[/size][/color][/heading]

Tempered, Hardened, and Crucible scale material matchups, combat pressure, recovery, and progression as one coherent ruleset. The table below shows their main differences; individual systems can still be adjusted or disabled.

[code]
| Modifier                        | Tempered | Hardened    | Crucible    |
|---------------------------------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Material-rule intensity         | 55%      | 100%        | 135%        |
| Outgoing player damage          | x0.95    | x0.90       | x0.85       |
| Incoming player damage          | x1.05    | x1.10       | x1.15       |
| Kill, quest, and proficiency XP | x0.95    | x0.90       | x0.85       |
| Added weak-spot base damage     | +10%     | +20%        | +30%        |
| Player and hostile arrow speed  | x1.10    | x1.30       | x1.50       |
| Player arrow gravity            | x0.75    | x0.75       | x0.75       |
| Hostile archer aim scatter      | 1.50 m   | 1.25 m      | 1.00 m      |
| Hostile enemy sight distance    | x1.10    | x1.30       | x1.50       |
| Hero footstep hearing range     | x1.10    | x1.20       | x1.30       |
| Native combat aggro persistence | x1.00    | x1.10       | x1.20       |
| Light/agile enemy movement      | x1.00    | Up to x1.05 | Up to x1.10 |
| Stamina and mana usage          | x1.00    | x1.05       | x1.10       |
| Potion Poisoning per class      | 60       | 65          | 70          |
| Standard food health rate       | x0.50    | x0.375      | x0.25       |
| Standard food health duration   | x4.00    | x4.00       | x4.00       |
| Discrete food stamina / second  | 1        | 1           | 1           |
| Native armor-weight penalties   | x1.00    | x1.05       | x1.10       |
| Light armor movement            | x1.00    | x1.025      | x1.05       |
| Medium physical armor           | x1.00    | x1.05       | x1.10       |
| Heavy/Overload physical armor   | x1.00    | x1.10       | x1.20       |
| Enemy attack slots              | +0       | +1          | +2          |
| Enemy attack recovery time      | x1.00    | x0.95       | x0.90       |
| Player poise damage dealt       | x1.00    | x0.95       | x0.90       |[/code]

[b]Tempered[/b] stays close to vanilla pressure while retaining lighter material rules and improved projectile speed. [b]Hardened[/b] is the intended default. [b]Crucible[/b] makes enemy knowledge, preparation, and armor roles matter most.

Progression follows the same curve: kill, quest, objective, and proficiency experience are reduced by preset, creating a slower and more deliberate journey. Coin rewards remain unchanged.

Outgoing and incoming player damage each have one toggle, and their exact values come directly from the selected preset. [b]WeakSpotDamageBonus[/b] and [b]HostileArcherAimScatter[/b] change with the preset, remain freely adjustable afterward, and can be set to 0 to disable their individual effects. [b]PlayerArrowGravityMultiplier[/b] remains independent from presets and defaults to 0.75. Every global modifier has its own control, and [b]DifficultyModifiersEnabled=false[/b] disables the complete supporting difficulty layer without disabling material combat or damage feedback.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Broad in Scope, Lightweight in Implementation[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone creates difficulty through small adjustments to systems Tainted Grail already uses:

[list]
[*]No enemy health inflation.[/*]
[*]No early-game Tenacity; its preset-independent curve begins after level 20.[/*]
[*]No behavior-tree or encounter rewrites.[/*]
[*]No global replacement of enemy templates.[/*]
[*]No permanent NPC stat changes stored in saves.[/*]
[*]Temporary model-owned stat tweaks are removed when actors or settings stop qualifying.[/*]
[*]Hostile awareness and combat movement share one lightweight reconciliation of loaded NPCs rather than continuous scanning.[/*]
[*]Every supporting modifier can be disabled independently.[/*]
[/list]

The result is broader and more consequential than a resistance-only mod while remaining predictable, modular, and close to vanilla architecture.

[code]BepInEx/plugins/SteelAndBone/SteelAndBone.dll[/code]

Incompatible config updates create a backup, regenerate safe defaults, and automatically restore compatible values you customized. FoA Mod Manager's final [b]Import Previous Settings[/b] tab remains available for conservative manual recovery. Restart after importing manually.

[center][color=#C94A4A][size=6]Steel and Bone[/size][/color]
[i]Bone, flesh, stone, and spirit. Know your enemy. Strike with purpose.[/i][/center]

[line]

[b]Steel and Bone[/b] is a broad, knowledge-driven difficulty mod built from lightweight, native-first, modular changes.

Its defining feature is a material weakness and resistance system. Bone does not fight like flesh. Stone does not react like an armored human. Spirits, drowned corpses, infected bodies, sea creatures, and living flora each reward different physical or magical answers.

Vanilla weaknesses, resistances, and immunities remain first in the damage pipeline. Steel and Bone reads the enemy and the incoming damage, respects what Tainted Grail already decided, and adds a focused rule only where it improves the matchup. Mixed hits are resolved by damage part, so physical, elemental, and status payloads keep their own reactions. Reactive damage numbers make the result immediately readable.

The wider difficulty layer grows outward from that same idea: preparation should matter. Enemies notice sooner, groups apply steadier pressure, arrows travel faster and flatter, armor creates clearer roles, and resources and advancement demand more commitment. These systems reinforce the matchup game without replacing enemy AI, rewriting encounters, or inflating health bars.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Combat Philosophy[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone follows one central rule: no combat style should answer every opponent. Arrows are deadly against exposed flesh and lightly protected casters, elemental magic turns armor's material against it through effects such as heated plate and electrified metal, and heavy armor and shields resist archery.

This triangle is the clearest expression of a wider material system rather than a rigid class restriction. Blunt weapons shatter bone and rigid constructs, blades and fire tear through flora, electricity shocks drowned and sea-dwelling enemies, and spirits resist ordinary weapons and biological effects. Good matchups provide a meaningful advantage, but player skill, equipment, and build choices still matter.

This philosophy is inspired by [url=https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/60888]Requiem - The Roleplaying Overhaul's[/url] lethal emphasis on coherent rules, preparation, and intelligent tactical play. Steel and Bone translates those ideas into Tainted Grail's native combat systems; it is not an attempt to reproduce the scope of a total conversion. It aims to be lightweight and modular above all.

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Know Your Enemy[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone classifies enemies through reachable runtime and template evidence before using editable name terms as fallback. Stronger and more specific families take precedence over broad categories, and elite enemies soften custom extremes without losing their identity.

[code]
| Enemy family      | Better answers          | Poor answers                               |
|-------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Bone undead       | Blunt                   | Blood, bleed, slash, pierce                |
| Constructs/stone  | Blunt                   | Blood, bleed, poison, edges                |
| Armored humanoids | Blunt, spells           | Slash, untyped physical, arrows vs Heavy   |
| Ordinary flesh    | Blood, poison, edges    | Mostly neutral; deliberately mild rules    |
| Flesh undead      | Fire, blunt             | Blood, bleed, poison, pierce               |
| Wyrd creatures    | Non-Wyrdness tools      | Wyrdness                                   |
| Drowned corpses   | Electric, blunt         | Blood, bleed, pierce                       |
| Infected flesh    | Fire, slash, arrows     | Poison                                     |
| Sea creatures     | Electric, slash, arrows | Cold                                       |
| Spirits           | Wyrdness                | Biological and ordinary physical attacks   |
| Flora and fungus  | Fire, slash             | Poison, bleed, pierce, arrows              |
| Swarms            | Area and elemental hits | Individual arrow impacts                   |[/code]

Exact strength depends on the preset and on the enemy's vanilla data. A specific vanilla reaction can be preserved or amplified instead of receiving a duplicate overlay. True immunities stay intact.

Generic physical attacks can use Tainted Grail's weapon identity as evidence: swords and axes lean slashing, daggers, polearms, and bows lean piercing, and blunt weapons remain bludgeoning. This keeps weapon choice legible even when a hit arrives without a specific physical subtype.

[b]Humanoid armor[/b]

Equipped armor creates a consistent physical matchup curve on Hardened:

[code]
| Attack type      | Exposed | Light | Medium | Heavy |
|------------------|---------|-------|--------|-------|
| Arrows           | x1.20   | x1.08 | x1.00  | x0.75 |
| Pierce           | x1.06   | x1.03 | x1.00  | x0.90 |
| Slash            | x1.04   | x0.98 | x0.92  | x0.82 |
| Blunt            | x1.00   | x1.00 | x1.08  | x1.15 |
| Generic physical | x1.00   | x0.98 | x0.94  | x0.88 |[/code]

Slash loses effectiveness faster than Pierce, while maces and other blunt weapons become better answers as armor gets heavier. If vanilla numerical armor is already reducing a hit, Steel and Bone softens its added resistance so the two systems do not compound into impractical damage.

[b]Arrow identity[/b]

Direct player arrows are evaluated as arrows rather than generic Pierce.

[code]
| Other Hardened target   | Physical arrow damage |
|-------------------------|-----------------------|
| Other favorable flesh   | x1.10 to x1.15        |
| Flesh undead / drowned  | x0.85                 |
| Flora / wood body       | x0.60                 |
| Spirits                 | x0.55                 |
| Constructs / stone body | x0.50                 |
| Swarms                  | x0.35                 |
| Confirmed skeletons     | x0.20                 |[/code]

Ordinary humanoid armor is read from equipped native item data. Tempered and Crucible apply the same identities at 55% and 135% rule intensity. Only the physical share receives the arrow matchup, so a Fire or Electric payload keeps its own material reaction.

[b]Spell identity[/b]

Magic follows material identity rather than acting as universal armor penetration.

[code]
| Armor tier | Direct spell base on Hardened |
|------------|-------------------------------|
| Light      | x1.02                         |
| Medium     | x1.07                         |
| Heavy      | x1.12                         |[/code]

Steel and Bone also reads the equipped armor's native Fabric, Leather, or Metal surface. Fire remains useful against all three, Electricity is strongest against Metal, and Cold gains a small edge against Metal. Blood, Wyrdness, biological effects, and spells that already ignore armor do not receive a duplicate armor-tier bonus. Vanilla subtype reactions still take priority.

Built-in floating damage numbers turn hidden matchup rules into visible information:

[list]
[*]Neutral hits use an amber-yellow baseline.[/*]
[*]Resisted hits become smaller and greyer.[/*]
[*]Weakness hits become larger and warmer.[/*]
[*]Critical hits and weak spots receive additional pop.[/*]

Size, color contrast, font, duration, drift, damage-over-time height, and maximum active numbers remain configurable.

[b]Enemy awareness and pressure[/b]
[list]
[*]Hostile sight distance rises by preset while native line of sight, visibility, hearing, alert buildup, and pursuit remain in control.[/*]
[*]Higher presets add simultaneous enemy attack slots and shorten recovery before those slots return.[/*]
[*]In combat, ordinary agile enemies gain up to 5% movement on Hardened or 10% on Crucible. Medium-armored, Elite, Beholder, and Slugholder enemies receive at most half. Heavy-armored enemies, bears, constructs, flora, bosses, minibosses, scripted Critters, and non-pathing actors retain their vanilla speed.[/*]
[*]Friendly actors, summons, allies, inactive AI, and dead actors are excluded from sight tuning.[/*]

[b]Projectile combat[/b]
[list]
[*]Player and hostile arrows travel at x1.10 / x1.30 / x1.50 speed by preset.[/*]
[*]Player arrows use x0.75 gravity by default on every preset. The value is independently configurable from x0.25 to x1.00 and never tilts the launch direction.[/*]
[*]Hostile velocity is applied before native movement prediction and ballistic aiming.[/*]
[*]Direct player arrows receive a material matchup on their physical share; hostile arrow damage remains native.[/*]
[*]Native aim, projectile offsets, collision, draw-strength scaling, elemental payloads, and bow animations remain unchanged.[/*]
[/list]

[b]Armor roles[/b]

[code]
| Player armor state | Role                        | Tempered / Hardened / Crucible |
|--------------------|-----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Light              | Movement                    | x1.00 / x1.025 / x1.05         |
| Medium             | Physical protection         | x1.00 / x1.05 / x1.10          |
| Heavy / Overload   | Strong physical protection  | x1.00 / x1.10 / x1.20          |
| Native penalties   | Weight and resource burden  | x1.00 / x1.05 / x1.10          |[/code]

Armor proficiency, native tier thresholds, individual penalty rules, and Overload behavior remain authoritative. Magical armor checks are unchanged.

[b]Passive shield protection[/b]

An equipped and readied shield provides modest passive protection against direct physical attacks from the front even when it is not actively raised. The shield's effective vanilla Block rating determines the reduction, while its BlockAngle determines the forward arc and is capped at 180 degrees total coverage.

[code]
| Effective Block | Tempered | Hardened | Crucible |
|-----------------|----------|----------|----------|
| 0               | 0.0%     | 0.0%     | 0.0%     |
| 10              | 0.8%     | 1.0%     | 1.2%     |
| 20              | 1.6%     | 2.0%     | 2.4%     |
| 30              | 2.4%     | 3.0%     | 3.6%     |
| 40              | 3.2%     | 4.0%     | 4.8%     |
| 50              | 4.0%     | 5.0%     | 6.0%     |
| 60              | 4.8%     | 6.0%     | 7.2%     |
| 70              | 5.6%     | 7.0%     | 8.4%     |
| 80              | 6.4%     | 8.0%     | 9.6%     |
| 90              | 7.2%     | 9.0%     | 10.8%    |
| 100             | 8.0%     | 10.0%    | 12.0%    |[/code]

Effective Block includes the game's normal requirement penalty. Rear attacks, direct magic, status effects, damage over time, falls, traps, and environmental damage receive no passive protection. Active blocking remains fully vanilla and never receives the passive reduction a second time.

[b]Tempered[/b] stays close to vanilla pressure while retaining lighter material rules and improved projectile speed. [b]Hardened[/b] is the intended default. [b]Crucible[/b] makes enemy knowledge, preparation, and armor roles matter most.

[b]PlayerDamageDealtMultiplier[/b] is independent from presets and defaults to 1. [b]PlayerArrowGravityMultiplier[/b] is also independent from presets and defaults to 0.75. Every global modifier has its own toggle, and [b]DifficultyModifiersEnabled=false[/b] disables the complete supporting difficulty layer without disabling material combat or damage feedback.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Broad in Scope, Lightweight in Implementation[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone creates difficulty through small adjustments to systems Tainted Grail already uses:

[list]
[*]No enemy health inflation.[/*]
[*]No behavior-tree or encounter rewrites.[/*]
[*]No global replacement of enemy templates.[/*]
[*]No permanent NPC stat changes stored in saves.[/*]
[*]Temporary model-owned stat tweaks are removed when actors or settings stop qualifying.[/*]
[*]Hostile awareness and combat movement share one lightweight reconciliation of loaded NPCs rather than continuous scanning.[/*]
[*]Every supporting modifier can be disabled independently.[/*]
[/list]

The result is broader and more consequential than a resistance-only mod while remaining predictable, modular, and close to vanilla architecture.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Install and Update[/size][/color][/heading]

Install with Vortex as a BepInEx plugin, or place the plugin folder at:

[code]BepInEx/plugins/SteelAndBone/SteelAndBone.dll[/code]

Incompatible config updates create a backup, regenerate safe defaults, and automatically restore compatible values you customized. FoA Mod Manager's final [b]Import Previous Settings[/b] tab remains available for conservative manual recovery. Restart after importing manually.

[center][color=#C94A4A][size=6]Steel and Bone[/size][/color]
[i]Bone, flesh, stone, and spirit. Know your enemy. Strike with purpose.[/i][/center]

[line]

[b]Steel and Bone[/b] is a small BepInEx 5 Mono knowledge-based difficulty mod for [b]Tainted Grail: The Fall of Avalon[/b]. It expands the game's existing enemy weakness and resistance system instead of replacing it.

Tainted Grail already gives some enemies hand-authored damage reactions. Skeletons, for example, already have a vanilla blunt weakness. Steel and Bone keeps those vanilla rules first, can amplify them by preset, and then adds its own material-based rules where the same damage type is still neutral.

[b]Tempered[/b] is the Vanilla+ preset: it stays close to the base game and leaves vanilla enemy multipliers unchanged by default. [b]Hardened[/b] and [b]Crucible[/b] make weaknesses and resistances sharper, so the higher you set the preset, the more important it becomes to read the enemy and choose the right damage type.

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Features[/size][/color][/heading]
[list]
[*]Expands Tainted Grail's vanilla enemy weakness and resistance system without rewriting combat, enemy AI, armor, or encounters.[/*]
[*]Three rule-strength presets: Tempered, Hardened, and Crucible.[/*]
[*]Tempered is the Vanilla+ preset: lighter Steel and Bone rules, with vanilla enemy multipliers unchanged by default.[/*]
[*]Hardened and Crucible make enemy weaknesses and resistances matter more by amplifying non-neutral vanilla enemy subtype multipliers.[/*]
[*]General damage rules can apply weaknesses as well as resistances.[/*]
[*]Vanilla-aware subtype checks avoid blindly stacking custom overlays on hand-authored enemy multipliers.[/*]
[*]Metadata-first target classification uses reachable template/runtime signals such as HitBones, HitStone, WyrdnessBound, SarrasCreature, ReefboundBody, Ghost, Scourge, Zombie, Bloody, Human, Humanoid, Bandit, and Cultist before falling back to display-name terms.[/*]
[*]Elite, MiniBoss, Boss, and Type:Elite metadata can flag elite-class targets, reducing custom Steel and Bone weakness bonuses and flooring custom resistances.[/*]
[*]Broad metadata such as HitFlesh, HitMagic, Monster, level, tier, and boss status stays neutral unless a more specific family signal is present.[/*]
[*]Generic physical hits can fall back to Tainted Grail's own weapon identity, so swords, daggers, polearms, bows, axes, and blunt weapons still have readable lanes when a hit is otherwise untyped.[/*]
[*]Bone undead, constructs, armored humanoids, ordinary flesh, flesh undead, Wyrd enemies, drowned enemies, infected flesh, sea creatures, spirits, and flora have editable target-family term lists.[/*]
[*]Recognizes physical subtypes, Fire, Cold, Poison, Electric, Wet, Wyrdness, Burn, Bleed, and blood-magic text cues.[/*]
[*]Built-in floating damage numbers for outgoing player hits, with #E3BD02 neutral color, smaller/desaturated resistance numbers, warmer/larger weakness numbers, critical pop scaling, and a font mode that can follow or force the game's Sans and Serif styles.[/*]
[*]Event-driven damage hook with cached target classification; no enemy scanning.[/*]
[/list]

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Built-In Damage Numbers[/size][/color][/heading]
Steel and Bone includes its own configurable floating damage-number system. Outgoing player hits show final damage values directly in the world: neutral hits use an amber-yellow baseline, resisted hits appear smaller and duller, weakness hits grow warmer and redder, and critical or weak-spot hits get extra pop. By default, damage numbers follow the game's Accessibility font choice; the config can also force the simple Sans font, the stylized Serif font, or Unity's IMGUI fallback font.

This is meant to replace separate combat-text overlays for Steel and Bone feedback. To avoid duplicate numbers, disable other damage-number mods such as [b]DamageNumbers.dll[/b] or Immersive HUD's damage text option.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Preset Model[/size][/color][/heading]
[code]
| Preset   | Meaning                                 |
|----------|-----------------------------------------|
| Tempered | Vanilla+; close to the base game        |
| Hardened | Intended default; matchups matter more  |
| Crucible | Strongest; enemy knowledge matters most |[/code]

Presets change intensity, not the list of matchups. Every Steel and Bone overlay has one base multiplier, and the preset scales that multiplier toward or away from neutral. Vanilla-authored enemy multipliers can also be amplified: Tempered defaults to 0% extra distance from neutral, Hardened to 35%, and Crucible to 70%, with true vanilla immunities left intact and extremes clamped.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Configuration[/size][/color][/heading]
Start the game once to generate:

[code]
BepInEx/config/ks.tgfoa.steel-and-bone.cfg[/code]

Common entries:

[code]
Enabled = true
Preset = Hardened
RespectVanillaMultipliers = true
AmplifyVanillaMultipliers = true
TemperedVanillaAmplification = 0
HardenedVanillaAmplification = 0.35
CrucibleVanillaAmplification = 0.7
EliteRuleClampsEnabled = true
EliteWeaknessBonusReduction = 0.1
EliteMinimumResistanceMultiplier = 0.2
DamageNumbersEnabled = true
DamageNumberBaseColor = #E3BD02
DamageNumberFontSize = 34
DamageNumberFontMode = GameDefault
DamageNumberHorizontalDrift = 1
DamageNumberVerticalDrift = 1
DamageOverTimeNumberHeightMultiplier = 1.25
DamageNumberSizeContrast = 1
DamageNumberColorContrast = 1
Diagnostics = false[/code]

Steel and Bone draws its own damage-number feedback for outgoing player hits
when [b]DamageNumbersEnabled[/b] is true. To avoid duplicate combat text,
disable other damage-number overlays such as [b]DamageNumbers.dll[/b] or
Immersive HUD's damage text option. [b]DamageNumberFontMode[/b] can be
[b]GameDefault[/b], [b]Sans[/b], [b]Serif[/b], or [b]ImguiDefault[/b].
[b]DamageNumberHorizontalDrift[/b] and [b]DamageNumberVerticalDrift[/b]
independently scale movement from 0 (off) through 1 (default) to 3
(exaggerated). Set both to 0 for numbers that pop and fade near the hit
position.
[b]DamageOverTimeNumberHeightMultiplier[/b] controls the initial height of
Bleed, Poison, Burn, and Breath status-tick numbers. Its default of 1.25 starts
those numbers 25% higher than ordinary damage numbers.
[b]DamageNumberSizeContrast[/b] independently controls resistance shrinking
and weakness growth. [b]DamageNumberColorContrast[/b] independently controls
resistance grey and weakness red-orange tinting. Both use 0 for no contrast,
1 for the default look, and values up to 3 for a more dramatic difference.
Critical and weak-spot pop remain independent, and immunity styling is
unchanged.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Updating[/size][/color][/heading]
Incompatible config updates create a backup and generate current defaults. Some supported settings are automatically carried over.

FoA Mod Manager's final [b]Import Previous Settings[/b] tab shows the current and available backup schemas. Toggle the import action to restore compatible customized settings. Restart the game after importing.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Notes[/size][/color][/heading]
This is still a compact combat-identity mod, not a total overhaul. It is meant to make enemy material, magic type, and weapon choice easier to read through combat outcomes while staying close to vanilla systems.

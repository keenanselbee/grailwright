[center][color=#C94A4A][size=6]Steel and Bone[/size][/color]
[i]Bone, flesh, stone, and spirit. Know your enemy. Strike with purpose.[/i][/center]

[line]

[b]Steel and Bone[/b] is a lightweight, native-first difficulty overhaul built around one idea: knowing your enemy should matter.

At its core, the material system gives bone, flesh, stone, spirit, armor, and other identities distinct reactions to physical, elemental, and status damage. Tainted Grail's native weaknesses, resistances, and immunities remain authoritative; Steel and Bone adds focused rules only where they sharpen the matchup.

Around that foundation, optional systems improve combat feedback, enemy pressure, projectiles, armor and shields, stamina, food and potions, and late-game challenge. Together they reward preparation and strong builds without replacing enemy AI, rewriting encounters, or inflating health bars.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Combat Philosophy[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone follows one central rule: no combat style should answer every opponent. Arrows are deadly against exposed flesh and lightly protected casters, elemental magic turns armor's material against it through effects such as heated plate and electrified metal, and heavy armor and shields resist archery.

This triangle is the clearest expression of a wider material system rather than a rigid class restriction. Blunt weapons shatter bone and rigid constructs, blades and fire tear through flora, electricity shocks drowned and sea-dwelling enemies, and spirits resist ordinary weapons and biological effects. Good matchups provide a meaningful advantage, but player skill, equipment, and build choices still matter.

This philosophy is inspired by [url=https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/60888]Requiem - The Roleplaying Overhaul's[/url] lethal emphasis on coherent rules, preparation, and intelligent tactical play. Steel and Bone translates those ideas into Tainted Grail's native combat systems; it is not an attempt to reproduce the scope of a total conversion. It aims to be lightweight and modular above all.

[line]

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Know Your Enemy[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone reads each enemy's body, armor, creature family, and specific identity. Specific enemies take precedence over broad families, while elites soften extreme custom matchups without losing their defining strengths and weaknesses.

[code]
| Family             | Weaknesses                      | Resistances                                |
|--------------------|---------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Skeletons / bone   | Blunt                           | Arrows; blood/bleed; slash/pierce; Cold    |
| Constructs / stone | Exact elemental counters; blunt | Blood/bleed/poison; Necrotic; arrows; Cold |
| Armored humanoids  | Blunt; direct spells            | Heavy arrows; slash                        |
| Ordinary flesh     | Arrows; blood/poison; blades    | No broad resistance                        |
| Flesh undead       | Fire; blunt                     | Necrotic; blood/bleed/poison; arrows       |
| Wyrd creatures     | Exact reactions vary            | Few broad resistances                      |
| Drowned corpses    | Electric; blunt                 | Necrotic; blood/bleed; arrows              |
| Infected flesh     | Fire; arrows; slash             | Poison                                     |
| Sea creatures      | Electric; arrows; slash/pierce  | Cold                                       |
| Spirits            | Wyrdness; Necrotic              | Biological attacks; ordinary physical      |
| Flora              | Axes; Fire/slash; Necrotic      | Arrows; poison/bleed/pierce                |
| Swarms             | Area and non-arrow attacks      | Individual arrows                          |[/code]

Exact strength depends on the preset and each enemy's native data. Existing reactions are preserved or strengthened rather than duplicated, and true immunities remain intact.

When [b]Soul and Service[/b] is installed, its complete rework of the original Soul Salvage spell is renamed [b]Soul Rend[/b]. Soul Rend's light cast deals exact Necrotic damage to living enemies. On Hardened, its matchups run from strongest resistance to strongest weakness: constructs x0.25, confirmed skeletons x0.40, flesh undead x0.60, drowned x0.675, infected bodies x0.85, Wyrd creatures x0.875, ordinary and sea flesh x1.10, flora or fungus x1.175, and spirits x1.225. The game's normal magical defenses still apply, while Soul Claim remains outside the Necrotic school.

When [b]Blood Magic Expansion[/b] is installed, its exact Blood Magic packets use a complementary Hardened matrix: skeletons and constructs x0.25, spirits x0.30, drowned and flora or fungus x0.65, flesh undead x0.75, Wyrd and infected bodies x0.85, sea flesh x1.10, and living flesh x1.15. This keeps Blood strongest into living bodies while Necrotic excels against spirits and unnatural growth. If the optional API is absent, Steel and Bone retains its conservative text fallback for other blood effects.

Body material and creature identity are resolved separately, so unusual enemies keep their intended physical reactions without inheriting an entire creature family's traits. Authored and exact profiles take priority, and Wyrd creatures receive no blanket resistance. These profiles cover important exceptions such as frost enemies resisting Cold but yielding to Fire, fire-aligned bodies yielding to Wet, drowned skeletons reacting to Electric, and soft-bodied creatures absorbing Blunt impact. Bone Undead and Constructs also resist Cold when otherwise neutral, though this does not make them immune to separate effects such as Chill.

Weapons follow their intuitive shape when an attack does not state a physical type: swords and axes slash; daggers, polearms, bows, and pickaxes pierce; and blunt weapons remain Blunt. Axes deal x1.20 damage to wood and flora on Hardened instead of stacking with the normal flora Slash weakness. Pickaxes keep their Pierce identity in combat and continue to mine normally.

[b]Humanoid armor[/b]

Equipped armor creates a consistent physical matchup curve on Hardened:

[code]
| Attack type      | Exposed | Light | Medium | Heavy |
|------------------|---------|-------|--------|-------|
| Arrows           | x1.20   | x1.08 | x1.00  | x0.75 |
| Pierce           | x1.06   | x1.03 | x1.00  | x0.90 |
| Slash            | x1.04   | x0.98 | x0.92  | x0.82 |
| Blunt            | x1.00   | x1.00 | x1.08  | x1.15 |
| Generic physical | x1.00   | x0.98 | x0.94  | x0.88 |[/code]

Edges lose effectiveness as armor becomes heavier, while blunt weapons become stronger answers. When native armor is already reducing a hit, Steel and Bone softens its added resistance so the two systems do not compound into impractical damage.


[b]Arrows and spells[/b]

Direct player arrows use their own material matchup instead of being treated as generic Pierce. On Hardened, favorable flesh receives x1.10 to x1.15 physical arrow damage, while resistant targets range from x0.85 for flesh undead and drowned enemies to x0.20 for confirmed skeletons. Flora, spirits, constructs, and swarms fall between those extremes. Only the physical share changes, so Fire, Electric, and other payloads keep their own reactions.

Magic follows material identity rather than ignoring armor. On Hardened, direct spells receive x1.02 / x1.07 / x1.12 against Light, Medium, and Heavy armor. Fire remains useful against Fabric, Leather, and Metal; Electricity is strongest against Metal; and Cold gains a smaller edge against Metal. Blood, Wyrdness, biological effects, and spells that already bypass armor receive no duplicate bonus. Native reactions remain authoritative.

Material impact keeps reactions proportional to effectiveness. Every resisted direct player hit carries 60% of its resistance into reduced poise and force, without letting weaknesses amplify control. Immune or very strongly resisted hits also lose the routine small flinch. Genuine poise breaks, force stumbles, ragdolls, damage processing, effects, and aggro remain intact.

Focused techniques provide fallback options without replacing material counters: pommel strikes count as Blunt against bone, stone, and armor; heavy melee attacks partially breach rigid resistance; and area hits pressure neutral swarms. Native reactions still take priority.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Readable Combat Feedback[/size][/color][/heading]


[b]Floating damage numbers[/b]

Built-in damage numbers make matchups easy to read: resisted hits appear smaller and greyer, weaknesses larger and warmer, immunities use distinct styling, and critical or weak-spot hits gain a red tint. Damage-over-time ticks appear higher and smaller to reduce clutter. Optional notice-only modes can replace numbers with RESISTED and IMMUNE text on every qualifying direct hit or only once per enemy.

[b]Effectiveness Feedback Sensitivity[/b] controls visual emphasis without changing damage. Confirmed weak spots add 10-30% base damage depending on the preset, while native critical damage remains unchanged. Size, color, font, timing, drift, damage-over-time display, and active-number limits are configurable.


[b]Optional crosshair hit markers[/b]

[url=https://www.nexusmods.com/taintedgrailthefallofavalon/mods/223]Dishonored Dynamic Crosshair[/url] 3.4.8 or newer can present the same final result at the center of the screen through eight states ranging from immunity and resistance through neutral damage to weakness.

Independent weak-spot, critical, damage-over-time, and killing-blow corpse-quality overlays remain supported. Dishonored can also distinguish the hero's direct attacks from damage dealt by hero-owned summons and optionally exclude the latter. Hit markers work without floating damage numbers and update immediately with each hit.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Expanded Combat Systems[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone raises combat pressure without replacing Tainted Grail's native AI or encounter design. Late-game enemies hold their ground longer, groups become more aggressive, projectiles pose a greater threat, and armor choices gain clearer roles. Each system remains independently configurable.


[b]Progressive Tenacity - Smoother late-game difficulty curve[/b]

Progressive Tenacity begins after level 20, giving regular enemies a small benefit while helping Elites, MiniBosses, and Bosses withstand burst damage, repeated staggers, and stamina pressure. Strong builds remain effective, and confirmed material weaknesses reduce Tenacity for that hit. It works with every preset, leaves the early game unchanged, and adds no inflated health bars or ability immunities.


[b]Enemy awareness and pressure[/b]

Enemies see and hear farther, retain combat attention longer, and coordinate more simultaneous attacks as the preset rises. Attack opportunities also return sooner, making groups less likely to wait passively while one enemy fights alone.

Hardened and Crucible give ordinary agile enemies a modest combat movement bonus. Heavy enemies, bosses, constructs, plants, bears, scripted creatures, and non-pathing actors retain their native speed. Line of sight, walls, chase boundaries, and individual enemy behavior continue to work normally.


[b]Projectile combat[/b]

Player and hostile arrows travel at x1.10 / x1.30 / x1.50 speed across Tempered, Hardened, and Crucible. Player arrows use x0.75 gravity on every preset and fly flatter; [b]PlayerArrowGravityMultiplier[/b] controls that preset-independent value. Hostile archers retain 1.50 / 1.25 / 1.00 meters of aim scatter; [b]HostileArcherAimScatter[/b] can override it. Archers become more immediate threats without gaining unnatural accuracy.

Native aiming, movement prediction, collision, draw strength, animations, and elemental payloads remain intact. Direct player arrows also receive Steel and Bone's material matchups, but only their physical damage is adjusted.


[b]Armor roles[/b]

Armor gains clearer strengths and tradeoffs:

[list]
[*][b]Light armor[/b] supports mobility and gains a small movement bonus on higher presets.[/*]
[*][b]Medium armor[/b] provides improved physical protection without the full burden of Heavy armor.[/*]
[*][b]Heavy armor[/b] provides the strongest physical protection but carries greater movement and resource costs.[/*]
[/list]

Native armor proficiency, weight classes, individual penalties, and Overload behavior remain authoritative. Magical armor checks are unchanged.


[b]Passive shield protection[/b]

A readied shield provides modest protection against direct physical attacks from the front, even when it is not actively raised. Protection scales with the shield's native Block rating and reaches up to 8% / 10% / 12% on Tempered, Hardened, and Crucible.

Active blocking remains fully native and never receives the passive reduction twice. Hidden shields, rear attacks, magic, status effects, damage over time, falls, traps, and environmental damage receive no passive protection.


[b]Player combat pressure[/b]

Across the presets, incoming damage rises while outgoing damage falls. Higher presets also increase stamina and mana costs and reduce repeated stagger pressure, making defense and resource management more important.

Positive mana regeneration during combat and accumulated positive parry-window bonuses use x1.00 / x0.75 / x0.50 across Tempered, Hardened, and Crucible. Mana Shield proportionally relieves only Steel and Bone's added regeneration penalty, while the game's native Mana Shield reduction and post-hit lock remain active. Parry scaling preserves the native 0.05-second base window and every non-positive total bonus. Both systems have separate toggles and adjustable multipliers.

Stamina Depleted presentation defaults to one smooth eased fade instead of the native repeating flash and abrupt disappearance. Native and Off modes remain available, and the choice changes presentation only; exhaustion audio, penalties, restrictions, and timing remain intact.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Preparation and Recovery[/size][/color][/heading]

Food provides sustained recovery before and between fights, while Potion Poisoning discourages repeatedly drinking the same type of potion under pressure. Together, these systems reward preparation without replacing Tainted Grail's native consumables.


[b]Food recovery[/b]

Food becomes sustained preparation instead of a rapid combat heal. Its native health effect remains in place, preserving the faded queued-healing display on the health bar, while Steel and Bone extends recovery over a longer period.

[list]
[*]Every preset extends standard food to four times its original duration.[/*]
[*]Tempered restores health at x0.50 rate for 200% total healing, Hardened at x0.375 for 150%, and Crucible at x0.25 for the original total.[/*]
[*]Qualifying food also restores exactly 1 stamina each second for the same duration, including during ordinary regeneration lockouts from actions.[/*]
[*]If you run out of stamina while food is active, its stamina recovery pauses, but Overexertion and Stamina Depleted last only half as long. Food stamina recovery resumes immediately when Overexertion ends, while health recovery and the food timer continue throughout.[/*]
[*]Selecting Tempered allows combat food use, while Hardened and Crucible prevent it. The setting remains freely customizable afterward, and noncombat food use is always unrestricted. Blocked attempts are silent unless Grail Floating Text is installed, which shows a brief food-styled explanation.[/*]
[*]Food effects cannot stack. Eating again keeps whichever active meal offers the greatest remaining health recovery, with remaining duration breaking a tie.[/*]
[*]Health and stamina recovery appear as one coherent food effect, and item descriptions show the adjusted result without adding mod or preset labels.[/*]
[/list]


[b]Potion Poisoning[/b]

Repeatedly drinking the same class of potion builds toward Potion Poisoning. Health, Mana, Stamina, and Utility potions each use their own buildup, so mixing different classes is safer than repeatedly relying on one resource.

[list]
[*]Two potions from the same class are safe. A third triggers poisoning when the first-to-third span is within 5 seconds on Tempered, 10 seconds on Hardened, or 15 seconds on Crucible.[/*]
[*]Health, Mana, and Stamina poisoning drain 30% of the corresponding maximum resource over the status duration.[/*]
[*]Utility poisoning drains 15% of maximum Health, Mana, and Stamina together. Buffs, cures, locks, regeneration potions, resets, and miscellaneous consumables share this class.[/*]
[*]A potion that restores more than one resource counts toward each matching class but can produce only one poisoning status.[/*]
[*]Triggering any class clears all four buildup pools. New buildup pauses while Potion Poisoning remains active.[/*]
[*]Normal healing and regeneration remain active and can offset the drain. Health poisoning is nonlethal and stops at 1 HP; Mana and Stamina can fall to zero.[/*]
[*]Potion restoration, unrelated effects, item text, and Better UI presentation otherwise remain native.[/*]
[/list]

Potion Poisoning uses the game's native status icon and duration. Class buildup clears gradually according to the preset, giving careful or varied potion use room without removing the danger of repeated reliance on one class.

[b]Tempered[/b] stays close to vanilla pressure while retaining lighter material rules and improved projectile speed. [b]Hardened[/b] is the intended default. [b]Crucible[/b] makes enemy knowledge, preparation, and armor roles matter most.

Progression follows the same curve: kill, quest, objective, and proficiency experience are reduced by preset, creating a slower and more deliberate journey. Coin rewards remain unchanged.

Each preset supplies coherent defaults rather than locking the player into them. Incoming and outgoing damage, weak-spot power, archer accuracy, arrow flight, resource pressure, armor, enemy aggression, recovery, and progression remain independently adjustable. The complete supporting difficulty layer can also be disabled without turning off material combat or damage feedback.

[heading][color=#C94A4A][size=5]Broad in Scope, Lightweight in Implementation[/size][/color][/heading]

Steel and Bone creates difficulty through small adjustments to systems Tainted Grail already uses:

[list]
[*]No enemy health inflation.[/*]
[*]No early-game Tenacity; its preset-independent curve begins after level 20.[/*]
[*]No behavior-tree or encounter rewrites.[/*]
[*]No permanent NPC stat changes stored in saves.[/*]
[*]Changes apply only while an actor or setting qualifies and clean themselves up automatically.[/*]
[*]Awareness and movement checks stay limited to relevant loaded enemies.[/*]
[*]Every supporting modifier can be disabled independently.[/*]
[/list]

The result is broader and more consequential than a resistance-only mod while remaining predictable, modular, and close to vanilla architecture.
